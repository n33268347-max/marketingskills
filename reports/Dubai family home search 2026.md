# Rent the Dip, Buy Villas Carefully in Dubai

As of late November 2026, the best strategy for Tom depends on his budget and how long he plans to stay. **Renting has become the stronger default for most families.** Apartment rents are falling by double digits, more than one in five renewals now come with a rent cut, and landlords of new townhouses in the outer suburbs are competing for tenants. **Buying makes sense mainly for a ready (already built) villa or townhouse in an established community, and only if Tom expects to stay at least five to seven years.** Villa prices barely fell in 2026 and are unlikely to drop much. Apartment prices are still drifting down, and a very large wave of new homes is due in 2027. The market was shaken by the Middle East / Iran war that began around March 2026, which caused the first price falls since 2020. Prices stopped falling in October, but S&P's base case is that the disruption continues into 2027. For value, the outer belt (DAMAC Hills 2, The Valley, Villanova, Town Square, Dubai South) offers the most space per dirham. Arabian Ranches 1-2 and DAMAC Hills offer the best value among mature communities with good schools. Dubai Hills, The Springs and Tilal Al Ghaf cost the most per square foot. **One important caveat:** nearly every figure in this report comes from agency blogs and press summaries, not from official records, and sources often disagree by 10-30% for the same community. Before Tom signs anything, he should check actual registered deals on the Dubai Land Department's (DLD) Dubai REST app, the rent cap on the DLD Smart Rental Index, and the building's service charge on the Mollak / Service Charge Index.

## Prices stopped falling in October, but apartments and villas are moving apart

2025 was a boom year. ValuStrat's index rose **19.8%** and villas rose **25.5%**, leaving average freehold villa values **206% above** post-pandemic lows ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-villa-prices-jump-206-since-the-pandemic-smashing-past-the-last-boom-1.500391148); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubais-residential-values-rise-almost-a-fifth-in-past-12-months)). 2026 went differently. In March, after the war began, ValuStrat recorded its first monthly fall since 2020, **-5.9%**, followed by eight months of small declines ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/dubai-residential-market-sees-volume-drop-and-moderate-price-correction-amid-middle-east-war/)). By August, prices were about **3.1% lower than a year earlier**. Apartments were down **5.3%** and villas only **1.7%** ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/6-in-10-dubai-homes-hold-their-value-as-market-cools-gently-in-august)). In October, the index ticked up **0.1%**. None of the 72 communities it tracks fell, and 19 rose ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-residential-values-post-first-monthly-gain-since-february)).

Not every source agrees on direction. Betterhomes' September medians show villas and townhouses **up 4.1%** year on year, at AED 1,638/sqft ([betterhomes](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-reports/dubai-property-market-report-q3-2026-secondary-sales-rose-16-as-leasing-demand-hit-a-record-high)). Cavendish Maxwell shows the whole market **down 1.7%**, at AED 1,636/sqft ([Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2026/real-estate/dubai-residential-sales-values-top-dhs72-6bn-across-34000-transactions-in-q3-26/)). The gap comes from method: a median of whatever happened to sell in a given month is not the same as an index that tracks comparable homes over time. S&P cites industry estimates of **5-15% falls** across segments since the end of 2025 ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/business/uae-realty-market/sp-assesses-impact-of-middle-east-war-on-dubai-property-market-1554183)). The most reasonable reading is this: **established villas are roughly flat (somewhere between -2% and +4% depending on the source), while apartments are about 5% lower.** Tom should not expect bargain prices on a family villa in a mature community.

Fewer deals are happening. Q3 2026 saw about **34,000 sales, 38% fewer than a year earlier** ([Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2026/real-estate/dubai-residential-sales-values-top-dhs72-6bn-across-34000-transactions-in-q3-26/)). Sellers had cut asking prices by a combined **AED 2.36bn across 3,292 listings** by the end of May ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/property-prices-down-dubai/)). Savills describes the market as having entered "a period of stabilisation" with "buyer selectivity" increasing ([Savills](https://www.savills.co.uk/insight-and-opinion/savills-news/396705/dubai-residential-market-enters-period-of-stabilisation-as-buyer-selectivity-increases)). In plain terms, buyers now have more leverage than at any time since 2020, but there is no fire sale.

Rents have shifted more clearly. Asking rents for apartments dropped sharply between Q3 2025 and Q3 2026: **Downtown -21.7%, Dubai Marina -16.1%, Business Bay -12.5% and JVC -10%**. Rental listings rose **55%** while renter enquiries rose only about **15%** ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/real-estate/dubai-rents-q3-2026); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-property-rental-prices-fall-where-they-were-once-the-highest-1.500714361)). The share of renewals that came with a rent cut jumped from **4.8% in Q1 to over 21% in Q3** ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-rents-1-in-5-tenants-now-pays-less-than-before-as-rentals-ease-on-new-supply)). Villa rents are harder to read. Property Finder shows asking rents falling in **8 of 10 villa communities**, led by Arabian Ranches 3 (**-18.9%**) and Dubai South (**-12.45%**), both areas where many new homes have just been handed over ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/real-estate/dubai-rents-q3-2026)). Other data shows rents still rising in mature communities: about **+3.6% in Arabian Ranches** and **+2.6% in Dubai Hills** ([FAM Properties](https://famproperties.com/blog/dubai-best-villa-communities-2026)). Rents on new villa contracts averaged about AED 200k, down from 225k, while renewing tenants saw rises of around 10% ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/real-estate/dubai-rents-q3-2026)). **The takeaway for Tom: as a new tenant he has leverage, especially in newer areas. Mature villa communities have softened the least.**

## A 2027 wave of new homes threatens apartments more than villas

About **38,000 homes were completed in January to September 2026**, more than in all of 2023 or 2024 ([Construction Week](https://www.constructionweekonline.com/analysis/dubai-delivers-38000-new-residential-units-in-first-nine-months-of-2026)). That is still far below the 100,000-120,000 that was "scheduled". Only about **48% of forecast units** actually completed in 2026, because developers routinely slip ([Truhauz](https://truhauz.com/dubai-handover-wave-2026-2027/)). Estimates for 2027 range from **60,000 (Cushman & Wakefield) to 162,500 (Cavendish Maxwell)** ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-real-estate-slower-handover-2025-2026); [Truhauz](https://truhauz.com/dubai-handover-wave-2026-2027/)). Nobody knows the true figure, but even half of the high estimate would be a record. In May 2025, Fitch predicted a correction of up to 15%. By June 2026 it was signalling that the fall could be deeper, because of the war's effect on tourism and population growth ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/property-prices-down-dubai/)). S&P expects a **gradual** decline, with **apartments falling faster than villas**, and housing stock growing about 20% through 2028 ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/business/uae-realty-market/sp-assesses-impact-of-middle-east-war-on-dubai-property-market-1554183)). UBS rates Dubai's bubble risk as "elevated" (1.16, 4th of 23 cities), but notes that risk has eased since March ([UBS](https://www.ubs.com/global/sc/media/display-page-ndp/en-20260922-grebi26.html)).

The new supply is concentrated in apartment-heavy areas. **JVC** (about 16,900 units scheduled for 2025-27), **Dubai South** (about 14,200 for 2026-27), **Business Bay** and **Al Furjan** are named most often as pressure points ([PropertyStellar](https://www.propertystellar.com/blog/dubai-property-handover-to-slow-in-2025-26-spike-in-2027-with-70k-units/); [Arabian Post](https://thearabianpost.com/dubais-rental-surge-faces-signals-of-moderation-in-2026/)). Mature villa communities such as Arabian Ranches 1-2, The Springs/Meadows, Jumeirah Golf Estates and Dubai Hills villas have little new supply coming. For Tom this has two sides. **If he is renting, the supply wave helps him**, so he should rent where it is landing. **If he is buying, he should avoid it** unless he gets a clear discount, because he would be selling later against a crowd of nearly identical investor-owned units.

This is the main reason **ready homes beat off-plan homes for a family.** Off-plan still makes up **72% of sales** ([Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2026/real-estate/dubai-residential-sales-values-top-dhs72-6bn-across-34000-transactions-in-q3-26/)). Yet developers have barely cut prices: only about **6% of projects cut prices by 5% or more since February** ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/dubai-developers-hold-off-plan-pricing-as-only-6-projects-cut-prices-by-5-or-more-since-february)). Meanwhile, sales of completed homes rose **16-18%** as buyers moved toward finished stock ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-ready-home-sales-jump-16-as-buyers-shift-away-from-off-plan-properties)). Off-plan payments go into a DLD escrow account under Law 8/2007. That protects Tom's money if the developer fails, but it **does not protect against delay** ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-want-to-invest-in-off-plan-property-red-flags-risks-to-watch-out-for-to-secure-dream-home)). Delays of 6-18 months are reported as common with weaker developers, though that claim comes from blogs ([Real Estate Club Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/buying-guide/dubai-off-plan-payment-plans-explained-a-buyers-guide)). Banks lend at most **50% of the value on off-plan homes** ([Datahabibi](https://datahabibi.ae/blog/dubai-mortgage-guide)). The typical payment plan is 10% at booking, 40% during construction and 50% at handover ([Real Estate Club Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/buying-guide/dubai-off-plan-payment-plans-explained-a-buyers-guide)), so Tom would be paying rent and instalments at the same time. Off-plan suits investors who can wait. It does not suit a family that needs a home and a school place now.

The official per-building figures are on the DLD's Mollak Service Charge Index ([Driven guide](https://www.drivenproperties.com/dubai-real-estate-market-guide/service-charge-index)), and Tom should look them up there rather than rely on agents.

## Schools and the morning drive narrow the map more than price does

School ratings are older than they look. The KHDA (Dubai's schools regulator) has **not issued new overall ratings since 2023-24**, because inspections were paused for two years. Every "Outstanding" list published in 2026 is therefore based on inspections about three years old ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/schools-and-parents/dubai-schools-new-khda-ratings-parent)). Fees at Outstanding schools for 2026-27 range from **AED 29,488 to AED 111,799** a year per child ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/schools-and-parents/dubai-school-fees-2026-27-tuition-costs-at-outstanding-schools-range)). With two children, fees can cost as much as the rent.

The good schools cluster in two places. The first is **Emirates Hills / Al Barsha / Al Sufouh**, which serves The Springs/Meadows, Al Barsha, Dubai Hills, JLT and Marina. Dubai British School and Dubai International Academy in Emirates Hills are both rated Outstanding ([bestschool.ae](https://bestschool.ae/articles/guides/outstanding-schools-in-dubai)), as is GEMS Wellington International at AED 47.5-103k ([Sotheby's Realty](https://sothebysrealty.ae/the-journal/schools-in-arabian-ranches/)). Kings' Dubai Hills is rated Very Good, at AED 48-72k ([Real Estate Club Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/dubai-life/best-international-schools-dubai-by-area-fees-curriculum-rankings-2026)). The second cluster is **Arabian Ranches / Dubailand**, which serves Arabian Ranches, DAMAC Hills, Mudon, The Villa and Tilal Al Ghaf. JESS Arabian Ranches is rated Outstanding by most sources, and Ranches Primary is Very Good at AED 47-62k ([Sotheby's Realty](https://sothebysrealty.ae/the-journal/schools-in-arabian-ranches/); [edcare](https://edcare.ae/schools/ranches-primary-school)). The cheap outer belt (DAMAC Hills 2, The Valley, Villanova, Emaar South) had **no sourced premium schools nearby**. Families there should expect a school commute. Waitlist data was not found, so Tom should call admissions offices before signing a lease.

Commutes are getting better slowly, but not in time for a 2026-27 move. **Hessa Street Phase 1 opened in April 2026.** It cut the Hessa-Al Khail trip from about 15 minutes to 3-4 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/04/19/upgrades-to-hessa-street-project-in-dubai-cut-traffic-time-from-15-to-four-minutes/)). Phase 2 was only about 10% complete in October ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/transport/dubai-hessa-street-phase-2-construction-progress-1.500740155)), and the Al Qudra Road upgrade, which matters for Arabian Ranches and its neighbours, is more than 60% complete ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/transport/dubais-biggest-future-road-projects-latest-rta-updates-completion-status-and-benefits-1.500600626)). The new metro lines are years away. The **Blue Line** (serving Mirdif and Creek Harbour) opens in **September 2029** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/transport/dubai-metro-blue-line-guide-routes-stations-key-features-and-opening-date-1.500157446)). The **Gold Line** (serving Dubai Hills, Al Barsha South, JVC and Jumeirah Golf Estates) opens in **September 2032** ([Dubai Media Office](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/april/22-04/mohammed-bin-rashid-approves-dubai-metro-gold-line)). Peak commutes of more than an hour are reported from eastern suburbs such as Silicon Oasis, and some Al Khail Road trips take 90 minutes ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-residents-blue-line-save-them-up-to-40-mins-of-daily-travel)). School-run traffic has been measured at **40 minutes for 2 km** ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/transport/school-traffic-rush-peak-hour-morning-runs)). The notes have no reliable drive times for individual communities. **Tom should test each shortlisted route in Google Maps at 7:30 am on a weekday, both to his office and to the school.** As a rough guide by location, Downtown, Al Barsha, Dubai Hills and Sobha Hartland are closest to DIFC. The Springs, Marina/JLT and Al Barsha are closest to Media and Internet City. Al Furjan, Emaar South and Jumeirah Golf Estates are closest to Dubai South / Expo.

Every community also carries some physical risk. In the **April 2024 storm**, Dubai received about 1.5 years of rain in 24 hours. **Mudon and Arabella** were hit worst, with residents evacuated by boat. Arabian Ranches and DAMAC Hills homes had collapsed ceilings, leaks and power cuts ([Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/gulf/2024/04/17/uae-floods-residents-left-counting-the-cost-of-damages-after-record-rainfall); [Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/gulf/2024/04/19/uae-floods-dubai-developers-offer-free-repairs-vow-action-after-record-rainfall)). **DAMAC Hills 2** has a record of maintenance and warranty complaints, including an AED 17,000 water bill caused by a failed valve after the warranty expired ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dh17000-water-bill-dubai-developer-urges-residents-to-take-preventative-measures?amp=1)). For any villa, Tom should ask the owner directly what damage it suffered in April 2024 and have the roof and waterproofing inspected.

## Buying wins only past about five years, and costs about 27% of the price in cash

**Upfront cost of buying a resale home with a mortgage is about 7-8% of the price.** That covers the 4% DLD transfer fee, about 2% agency commission plus VAT, a trustee fee of about AED 4,200, mortgage registration (0.25% of the loan plus AED 290), a bank fee of 0.5-1%, and a valuation of about AED 3,000 ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/cost-of-buying-property-in-dubai); [Mortgease](https://mortgease.ae/blog/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/)). Selling later costs about another 2%, so **buying and later selling costs about 10% in total** ([Real Estate Club Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/buying-guide/renting-vs-buying-property-in-dubai-2026-complete-10-year-cost-comparison)). Mortgage rules for resident expats follow the central bank's (CBUAE) regulations. Banks lend up to **80% of the value on a first home up to AED 5M, and 70% above that**. The limit is **50% for off-plan**, the maximum term is **25 years**, and total monthly debt payments cannot exceed **50% of income** ([CBUAE Rulebook](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/node/2850); [House and Hedges](https://houseandhedges.com/blog/dubai-mortgage-rules-2026-ltv-limits-rates-dbr-caps-what-buyers-need-to-know)). Non-residents typically get only 50-60% and pay rates 0.5-1 percentage points higher ([Mortgage Compare](https://mortgagecompare.ae/knowledge-hub/expat-mortgage-rates-uae.html)). In November 2026, fixed rates started around **3.89-4.19%**. Variable loans are priced at 3-month EIBOR (the Emirates interbank rate, about **4.28%**) plus a 1-2% margin, which works out to roughly 5.3-6.3% ([Angel in Dubai](https://www.angelindubai.com/post/dubai-mortgage-home-loan-rates-compared); [Mortgage Compare](https://mortgagecompare.ae/knowledge-hub/best-mortgage-rates-uae.html)). **A 3-5 year fixed rate is currently the better deal.** Tom should check what the rate becomes after the fixed period ends and what the bank charges for paying off early.

Here is a worked example for an **AED 4M villa with an 80% loan**. The fees come to about AED 276-292k. Add the AED 800k deposit, and Tom needs **about AED 1.08M in cash**, roughly 27% of the price. Above AED 5M, the lower 70% limit pushes this to about 37%. The same villa might rent for about AED 200k a year (a 5% gross yield). As an owner, Tom would pay about AED 128k in interest in year one, AED 20-40k in service charges and maintenance, and give up the return on AED 1M of cash. **His yearly unrecoverable costs as an owner end up similar to rent**, so the ~10% round-trip cost takes about **4-7 years** to earn back ([PSI](https://www.psinv.net/de/blog/renting-vs-buying-the-break-even-point-in-the-uae-market); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2026/07/11/uae-property-is-it-still-worth-buying-a-house-in-dubai-after-more-than-15-years-of-renting/)). If prices drift lower, it takes longer. Typical villa yields are **5-6.5% gross and 3.5-4.8% net** (in Arabian Ranches, for example) ([Oliva](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/arabian-ranches-dubai-villa-investment-guide)). Implied yields are lower in pricier areas, at about 4.0-4.3% in The Springs. In those areas buying is a weaker deal and renting a stronger one. One extra benefit of buying: a property worth **AED 2M or more** qualifies Tom for a **10-year Golden Visa**, and mortgaged homes are eligible ([Property Finder](https://help.propertyfinder.ae/hc/en-us/articles/37816365548946-How-to-get-a-UAE-Golden-Visa-through-Dubai-property)). Reports say that in early 2026 the rule requiring a minimum amount already paid off on a mortgaged property was dropped ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/news/uae-scraps-minimum-payment-for-property-golden-visa/)), but at least one official-looking page still requires AED 2M paid ([Wasl](https://www.wasl.ae/en/node/618)). Tom should confirm with the DLD before counting on it.

Renting has its own costs and protections. **Move-in costs come to about 10-15% of a year's rent.** That covers a 5% deposit (10% if furnished), about 5% agency fee plus VAT, a DEWA (utility) deposit of AED 4,000 for a villa, and Ejari (lease registration) of AED 120-235. On top of that, a **5% municipality housing fee** is added to the monthly DEWA bills ([Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/hidden-costs-renting-dubai/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/housing/dubai-rental-costs-ejari-dewa-deposits-moving-fees-1.500296020)). For an AED 300k villa, that is about AED 35-40k upfront. Once Tom is in, the law protects him. Increases are allowed only at renewal, with **90 days' written notice**, and are capped in bands tied to the **DLD Smart Rental Index**: no rise if the rent is within 10% of the index, up to 20% if it is more than 40% below ([Primadom](https://www.primadom.ae/blogs/dubai-rera-rent-calculator-2025-rental-index); [Splendor](https://splendor.ae/blogs/notice-period-for-rent-increase-in-dubai-what-tenants-and-landlords-need-to-know-in-2025/)). A landlord who wants to sell or move in must give **12 months' notice by notary or registered post** ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/landlord-eviction-notice-in-dubai-when-is-a-12-month-notice-valid)). The cap does not apply to the first year's rent, so the negotiation at signing matters most.

## Negotiate with sold prices, verify everyone, and pay nothing off the record

**When renting**, Tom should use the oversupply. JLL reports that landlords are "more willing to do a deal" and are offering more cheques and rent-free periods where many similar units are listed ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/06/26/dubai-rental-market-cools-as-supply-surge-hands-tenants-new-negotiating-power/)). Agency guides claim 1-3 months rent-free on two-year contracts and 5-10% off for paying in a single cheque. These figures are anecdotal ([Real Estate Club Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/renting/how-to-negotiate-rent-dubai-2026-market-data-scripts-timing-tips)). Tom should use registered rents in the building from the Smart Rental Index or Dubai REST as his evidence, not asking prices. He has the most leverage in newly handed-over townhouse clusters and in large apartments in Downtown, Marina, Business Bay and JVC, and the least in mature villa areas.

**When buying**, Tom should base his offer on DLD sold prices for the same layout over the last 3-6 months, pulled from Dubai REST ([Aigents Realty](https://aigentsrealty.com/blog/dubai-property-negotiation-tips-buyers-guide)). Gaps of 20% or more between asking and sold prices, as in The Springs, mean the asking price is not a useful guide. He should look for listings that have already been cut and sellers who need to sell. He can strengthen his offer by arriving with mortgage pre-approval, which is important because **failed financing is a common reason buyers default after signing** ([Driven Properties](https://www.drivenproperties.com/blog/mou-dubai-real-estate)). He should also negotiate terms as well as price: who pays for the developer's NOC (no-objection certificate, which costs AED 500-5,000 depending on the developer), service-charge arrears, handover condition and the completion date. The DLD's Form F sale contract usually requires a **10% security deposit cheque** ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/mou-agreement-dubai-real-estate)). Tom should have a lawyer review the clause on losing that deposit, and should make the deal conditional on the mortgage and the bank's valuation. A home that already has a tenant should sell at a discount, because Tom would need to give 12 months' notice before moving in.

**Scams are common, but the checks to avoid them are simple.** Every licensed agent has a BRN number and every agency an ORN, both checkable on the Dubai REST app. Every advert must carry a **Trakheesi permit number and a Madmoun QR code** ([UAE Expert Hub](https://www.uaeexperthub.com/verify-property-listing-dubai-avoid-scams/)). Common scams include fake agents who collect deposits and disappear, the same unit rented to several people, listings priced suspiciously low, and refusals to allow an in-person viewing or to register Ejari ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-rental-scam-agents-deposits-disappear); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/searching-for-a-home-dubai-police-alert-residents-to-new-rental-scam-online-1.500461271)). Tom should check the owner's name against the title deed and confirm that the person signing either owns the property or holds a valid power of attorney. He should pay only into traceable accounts and use official DLD channels, because some third-party "permit check" websites are themselves scams.

## Conclusion

The war and the coming supply wave have split Dubai's housing market in two. Apartments and newly built suburban stock are now a renter's market, while mature villa communities with schools still hold their value. For a family, that suggests a hybrid approach. **Tom can rent where supply is landing, to capture discounts now, and buy only where supply is scarce, so he isn't competing with investor flips in 2027-28.** A sensible plan for a family that is not yet sure how long it will stay is to sign a one- to two-year lease on a new-build townhouse at a negotiated discount. That leaves the purchase decision until after the 2027 handover peak, by which point the true scale of the wave and the path of the war should both be clearer.

The biggest gap in this research is the data itself. No source gave bedroom-level registered prices, community-level supply counts or verified service charges. Agency sources also tend to quote whichever figure flatters their stock. That is less a weakness of the research than a feature of the Dubai market. Tom's advantage over most buyers and renters will come from using the official DLD tools (REST, the Smart Rental Index and Mollak), which are free and give exact figures for any building he is considering.
