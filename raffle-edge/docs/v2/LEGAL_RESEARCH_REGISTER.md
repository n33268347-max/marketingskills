## Key legal conclusions by business model

These conclusions are cautious and provisional. Each one cites register IDs. The conclusions themselves are interpretation (class D) unless stated otherwise.

### (P) Player strategies and free postal entry
- **Free postal entry is a statutory feature, not an operator favour.** A paid draw stays outside the definition of "lottery" only if every eligible person can choose to enter by ordinary 1st or 2nd class post (or an equally cheap and convenient method), that choice is publicised, and the prize-allocation system treats free and paid entries the same (L08, L09, L07, G02, G03, G06). On that reading, a postal entry should carry exactly the same win chance as one paid ticket.
- **The stamp is not "payment"** (Sch 2 para 5, L07). A fee to claim a prize is payment (L10).
- **Free entry is underused.** Operators self-report that only about 6% of entries are free (D10, class B). Whether that creates a measurable value edge is an empirical question for the research. It is not a legal one.
- **Skill-only competitions have no statutory free route** (L11). Whether a site is genuinely skill-based depends on the s.14(5) test (L03, L04, G04, G07).
- **Regulatory-change risk:** the free-route and skill definitions can be changed by regulations (L05) and are named as possible targets for legislation (D11). The government has said the voluntary Code's success "will determine" further action including legislation (D06).
- **Marketing risk (high):** DMCCA Sch 20 para 18 bans in all circumstances "Claiming that products are able to facilitate winning in games of chance" (K07). Any paid tips, strategy tool or "edge" product must be cleared by a solicitor before it is marketed.

### (V) Draw-verification business
- **Existing rules create demand but set no standard.** DCMS Code 2.2 and CAP 8.24 require an "independent person", or a computer process producing "verifiably random" results (DCMS adds "and auditable") (D01, C06). CAP 8.25 requires an "independently audited statement" for instant wins (C07). Neither defines who is independent or what certification means.
- **Verification can test statutory conditions,** such as equal treatment of free entries (L09, G03) and free-route prominence (G02, C13). It can also test whether an operator's claims of Code compliance are accurate (K05, K06).
- **Third-party providers are expected to follow relevant Code requirements** (D02, D03). The Code's own signatory list shows established competitors, including PromoVeritas, The Fair Draw Register, UVS and OCSA (D07).
- Liability, insurance and the legal effect of an attestation are unresolved (see the open questions).

### (C) Comparison / value site
- **Biggest legal risk: s.252 "promotes a lottery".** Publishing promotional material for an operator, advertising it, or inviting people to enter its draw can count as promoting a lottery. If that operator's free route fails Sch 2 para 8, the draw is an illegal lottery and the site may be "promoting" it under s.252 (L14, L12). There is a reasonable-belief defence (L13), but it is untested for a listing site. Affiliate commission makes the risk worse. The GC lists defective free routes as a common problem (G08).
- **The site's own claims are regulated.** Its marketing is subject to CAP 3.1, 3.7, 3.34, 3.38 and 3.41 (C16-C20). Its promotion of other traders' products is within DMCCA ss.225-226 (K02-K04). Value and ranking methods must be stated, verifiable, and backed by documentary evidence. The project's archived-snapshot evidence model fits this well.
- **Do not imply regulatory approval.** The GC "does not approve" free draws (G01). Any DCMS signatory badge must be re-checked against the dated signatory list (K06).
- **Prize-value claims:** compare the advertised prize with any cash alternative, and watch for sales-threshold prizes (C03, C11, C12, K08).

### (D) Data collection
- **Database right is the main commercial risk.** It arises from substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting contents (R01). Extracting a substantial part infringes it, and so can "repeated and systematic" extraction of insubstantial parts (R02, R03). The reg 20 exception covers only non-commercial research by a lawful user, with the source cited (R04). It will not cover a comparison site or a paid service.
- **Winner names are personal data.** Promoters publish them only to prove a valid award, and winners can object (C08, C09). Analysis does not need names, so collecting them is unlikely to be "necessary" under the legitimate-interests test (P01, P02, P03). The current no-names design is the right default.
- **Computer misuse:** s.1 requires knowingly unauthorised access (M01). GET-only, no-login, robots-obeying collection that stops on any challenge keeps well clear of it. Website terms are a separate contract risk.

## Open questions for a solicitor

1. **s.252/s.258 exposure for a comparison or affiliate site.** Could listing, linking to or earning commission from an operator whose free route is defective count as "promoting" an illegal lottery? Would a documented due-diligence check support the s.258(4) reasonable-belief defence? (L12-L14)
2. **DMCCA Sch 20 para 18.** Would marketing a player-strategy, EV calculator or "edge" product (free or paid) be "claiming that products are able to facilitate winning in games of chance"? Is neutral statistical disclosure outside it? (K07)
3. **Sch 2 para 8(1)(d) and practical limits.** Do caps on postal entries per person or per envelope, stricter postal formatting rules, or postal-entry cut-offs earlier than online ones amount to "differentiation" or a "less convenient" route? (L08, L09)
4. **Database right in draw-results pages.** Is the investment in "obtaining/verifying/presenting" or in "creating" the data? What collection volume or frequency is safe for (a) the research phase and (b) any commercial product? Can "lawful user" status survive terms that forbid automated access? (R01-R04)
5. **Verification liability.** What liability (negligence, misrepresentation), insurance and engagement terms apply to acting as the "independent person", or to issuing an "independently audited statement" under CAP 8.25? Is any professional qualification implied? (D01, C06, C07)
6. **s.226(1)(d) and (5) and the DCMS Code.** Are any Code requirements firm enough ("no discretion") that an operator claiming compliance while breaching them commits a misleading action? (K05)
7. **Comparative advertising rules.** Do CAP 3.33-3.42 apply to a neutral third-party comparison site, or only to marketers comparing their own products? What defamation or malicious-falsehood safeguards are needed before publishing negative findings about named operators? (C18, C20)
8. **Personal data.** If winner data were ever needed (e.g. to test repeat-winner patterns), is pseudonymised or aggregate processing defensible under legitimate interests, and what transparency is required? (P01-P04)
9. **Website terms and the Computer Misuse Act.** Does ignoring robots.txt or terms (which this project does not do) risk CMA s.1, or only contract liability? (M01)
10. **Regulatory horizon.** Is any DCMS consultation, regulation under s.14(7), or Gambling Commission statement on prize draws pending after 19 Oct 2026? None was found here; that comes from a search, not proof of absence. (L05, D04-D06, D14)
11. **Scope.** This register covers Great Britain. Northern Ireland is governed by different law (ASA advice refers to the 1985 NI Order), and Scotland/Jersey/Guernsey entrants may need separate advice.
